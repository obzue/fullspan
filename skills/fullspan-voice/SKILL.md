# Voice

Language artifacts have a reader and a job. Everything that does not serve that job comes out.

## Assert, for words

Before sentences:

1. Reader: who they are and what they already believe.
2. Job: what they should be able to do or decide after reading.
3. Claim: one sentence you are willing to be wrong about.
4. Beats: the order of moves, as a list, not as paragraphs. Usually: situation, tension, evidence, ask.

If you cannot write the claim, you are still in Map.

## Draft

- Lead with the claim or the ask. Context earns its place after, not before, unless the reader will be lost without one fact.
- One idea per paragraph. Short sentences when the idea is hard. Longer ones when the relationship is the idea.
- Concrete nouns over abstractions. "The invoice failed" over "an issue was encountered in the billing flow."
- No throat-clearing, no "in today's world," no summary that repeats the previous paragraph in softer words.
- Jargon only if the reader shares it. If a word is doing status instead of work, cut it.
- Claims of fact carry a source (`fullspan-scout`) or are marked as your judgment.

## Cut

Read it aloud, or read only the first sentence of each paragraph. If those sentences do not tell the story, the middles are hiding the structure. Rebuild from the beats.

A second pass deletes:

- Adjectives that do not change a decision.
- Sentences that apologize for existing.
- Repeated points you kept because you liked the phrasing.

## Forms

- **Notes to a future self or agent:** decisions, constraints, and where things live. No pep talk.
- **A pitch:** reader, pain, offer, proof, ask. One page.
- **A lesson:** one skill, one example, one try. Do not survey a field.
- **A release note:** what changed for them, what did not, how to undo it.

## Done

A stranger can say the claim back, and the job in the promise is possible from the text alone.
