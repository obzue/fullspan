# Shelf

A map of books, not a library. Open `fullspan-shelf` before you assign one. Title and author are the lock. Scout the current edition before you cite a page or a price. Do not paste a chapter into this repo.

Checked against public reading lists on 2026-10-11. A later list can move a row. The code of this repo still beats a book.

## Computer science

| Level | Book | Open it when |
|---|---|---|
| First algorithms | Grokking Algorithms, Aditya Bhargava | You want pictures before proofs. |
| Practice | The Algorithm Design Manual, Steven Skiena | You need to pick an algorithm for a real problem. |
| Reference | Introduction to Algorithms, Cormen, Leiserson, Rivest, Stein | You need the proof and the standard name. |
| How the machine runs | Computer Systems: A Programmer's Perspective, Bryant and O'Hallaron | Code is right and the machine still surprises you. |
| Operating systems | Operating Systems: Three Easy Pieces, Remzi and Andrea Arpaci-Dusseau | Processes, memory, or disks. Free to read. |
| Networks | Computer Networking: A Top-Down Approach, Kurose and Ross | The path from the app down to the wire. |
| Data systems | Designing Data-Intensive Applications, Kleppmann, 2nd with Riccomini | Storage, replication, or a system you cannot hold on one box. |
| Craft | The Pragmatic Programmer, Hunt and Thomas | Habits, not a language. |
| Complexity | A Philosophy of Software Design, John Ousterhout | The design is larger than the job. |
| Models of computing | Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs, Abelson and Sussman | You want computation as an idea, not a syntax tour. Hard on purpose. |
| Math | Mathematics for Computer Science, Lehman, Leighton, Meyer | Proofs, counting, graphs. Free from MIT. |

## Computer engineering

| Level | Book | Open it when |
|---|---|---|
| First hardware picture | How Computers Really Work, Matthew Justice | Switches, gates, and what a program touches. |
| Systems from the bottom | Dive Into Systems, Matthews, Newhall, Webb | Architecture and the software sitting on it, together. |
| Digital design | Digital Design and Computer Architecture, Harris and Harris | Gates through a processor you can build. |
| Organization | Computer Organization and Design, Patterson and Hennessy | The RISC-V edition unless a course names another. |
| Advanced architecture | Computer Architecture: A Quantitative Approach, Hennessy and Patterson | You are measuring pipelines, caches, or cores. |
| Chip | CMOS VLSI Design, Weste and Harris | One book if the work is transistors and layout. |
| Embedded | Computers as Components, Marilyn Wolf | A device with a real processor, not a laptop essay. |
| Memory hardware | A Primer on Memory Consistency and Cache Coherence, Sorin, Hill, Wood | Cores disagree about what memory holds. This is not human memory. |

## Product design

| Level | Book | Open it when |
|---|---|---|
| Why objects fail | The Design of Everyday Things, Don Norman | Physical or digital. Start here. |
| Screens | Don't Make Me Think, Steve Krug | A page should be obvious. |
| Interaction | About Face, Cooper, Reimann, Cronin, Noessel | The behavior of the product, not the color. |
| Visual UI | Refactoring UI, Wathan and Schoger | The layout looks amateur and the structure is fine. |
| Alone on the team | The User Experience Team of One, Leah Buley | One person is the whole design desk. |
| Research | Just Enough Research, Erika Hall | You are about to ask people, and you need a small study. |

## Product marketing

| Level | Book | Open it when |
|---|---|---|
| Before the product | The Mom Test, Rob Fitzpatrick | Talk to customers without them lying to be kind. |
| Positioning | Obviously Awesome, April Dunford | People do not understand what it is. |
| After early fans | Crossing the Chasm, Geoffrey Moore | Early users love it and the mainstream does not buy. |
| Fit | The Lean Product Playbook, Dan Olsen | You need to see which layer of fit is broken. |
| Discovery | Continuous Discovery Habits, Teresa Torres | The team ships features and does not learn. |
| Product teams | Inspired, Marty Cagan | Marketing is sitting next to a feature factory. |

## Business and corporate management

| Level | Book | Open it when |
|---|---|---|
| The job of managing | The Practice of Management, Peter Drucker | You need the subject, not a slogan. |
| Your own week | The Effective Executive, Peter Drucker | Time, contribution, and decisions. |
| A team | High Output Management, Andy Grove | Output is the team's output, plus the teams you affect. |
| First months in a role | The First 90 Days, Michael Watkins | A new post, not a new personality. |
| Strategy | Good Strategy Bad Strategy, Richard Rumelt | The plan is a list of hopes. |
| Disruption | The Innovator's Dilemma, Clayton Christensen | The good product is losing to a worse one. |
| Systems | Thinking in Systems, Donella Meadows | A local fix keeps making the same mess. |
| The model | Business Model Generation, Osterwalder and Pigneur | You cannot say how the organization creates and keeps value. |

Good to Great is famous. Several of its companies later failed. Do not use it as a first management book.

## Memory and learning

These are about human memory. They do not replace `fullspan-recall`. Cache coherence is in computer engineering, above.

| Level | Book | Open it when |
|---|---|---|
| How study works | Make It Stick, Brown, Roediger, McDaniel | Retrieval beats rereading. This is why a lesson check closes the script. |
| The brain of learning | How We Learn, Stanislas Dehaene | Attention, engagement, error, and consolidation. |
| Accessible tour | How We Learn, Benedict Carey | A lighter pass before Dehaene. |
| Technical subjects | A Mind for Numbers, Barbara Oakley | The material is math, code, or circuits. |
| Expertise | Peak, Ericsson and Pool | Hours are not practice. Practice needs a target and feedback. |
| Technique, not study | Moonwalking with Einstein, Joshua Foer | Memory palaces. Interesting. Not how this house stores facts. |
| History | The Art of Memory, Frances Yates | Where the old memory arts came from. Not a procedure. |
